# EX GAMES — DESIGN BENCHMARK 001
## Competition, Participation, Media, Incentives, Monetisation and Narrative
### 13 August 2026

**Status:** Design benchmark / polished first draft  
**Purpose:** Preserve the design position reached in the 13 August 2026 session.  
**Important:** This is not yet a Lovable build specification. Public wording, scoring values and detailed mechanics remain provisional.

---

# 1. PURPOSE — ACCELERATE THE WORK

Great ecological restoration work is already happening throughout Aotearoa.

Ex Games exists to help it happen **faster, more widely and more effectively**.

The aim is to increase the pace, reach and effectiveness of ecological restoration by making existing work visible, bringing more people into action, recognising outstanding restoration, connecting fragmented efforts, creating incentives for sustained effective action, directing attention and resources toward useful work, and making ecological progress interesting enough for a much wider public to follow.

A major national reference point is **Predator Free 2050**. Ex Games is intended to help build the participation, momentum, visibility and public engagement capable of accelerating progress toward that ambition.

Predator Free 2050 is not the complete definition of Ex Games. Successful restoration also depends on invasive plants, habitat, waterways, wetlands, coastlines, biodiversity, ecological connectivity and neighbouring land management. A 200-hectare predator-control project may remain challenged if an important 200 m² invasive-plant problem next door is ignored.

Working thought:

> **Great work is already happening all over Aotearoa. Ex Games wants to help it happen faster.**

This is intent, not settled public copy.

# 2. WHAT EX GAMES IS

Final public wording is not settled.

Working propositions explored included:

> **Ex Games is a fun way to showcase great work.**

and:

> **Ex Games brings together and showcases the restoration work happening across Aotearoa.**

The underlying proposition is clearer than the wording:

**Ex Games makes ecological restoration visible, connected, engaging and recognised — and uses that visibility, connection and recognition to encourage more action and greater impact.**

People can see restoration activity, show what they are doing, follow people/projects/places, connect with others, represent their locality and region, receive recognition, find help and resources, help other participants, tell their stories and, if they choose, qualify to compete.

Participation in Ex Games does **not** require competition.

# 3. EX GAMES AND THE GAMES

**Ex Games** is the broader platform, network, media property and ecological participation ecosystem.

**The Games** are the competitive mechanism within Ex Games.

Current working definition:

> **The Games are a way to highlight and recognise outstanding restoration.**

Ex Games can recognise and connect everyone doing legitimate ecological work. The Games identify and recognise outstanding ecological performance.

A person or organisation does not need to become a Competitor to belong to Ex Games or make a meaningful contribution.

# 4. PARTICIPATION PROGRESSION

Current working progression:

**LOCATED → CONNECTED → ACTIVE → QUALIFYING → COMPETITOR → REGIONAL FINALIST → NATIONAL FINALIST → CHAMPION**

**Located:** Ex Games has independently identified legitimate ecological work and placed that work on the Living Map.

**Connected:** The person/group has claimed or confirmed its presence and joined the network.

**Active:** The participant is sharing or recording current ecological activity.

**Qualifying:** The participant wants to compete and is demonstrating sufficient ecological relevance, consistency, skill/impact and evidence to earn Competitor status.

**Competitor:** Earned status. It cannot simply be bought. Competition is voluntary and open in principle to anyone capable of qualifying.

**Regional Finalist / National Finalist / Champion:** Competitive progression through the season.

This supports sporting narratives: rookies coming through the ranks, emerging competitors, seasoned professionals, returning finalists, breakthrough performers, regional champions and national contenders.

# 5. THE LEVELLING PRINCIPLE

Individuals must be capable of competing meaningfully against large organisations.

One person might clear 200 m² of strategically important invasive ginger while a large organisation establishes predator control across 200 hectares. Hectares, dollars, volunteer numbers, plants planted or pests killed cannot by themselves establish which was the more outstanding ecological performance.

Ex Games should evaluate **performance in ecological context**, not simply quantity.

> **The size of the action does not necessarily determine the size of the impact.**

# 6. COMPETITION PERFORMANCE ARCHITECTURE

Four headline judging dimensions are currently preferred.

## 1. Ecological Significance
**How important was the problem or opportunity addressed?**

Consider urgency, ecological value, threat severity, strategic importance, vulnerability and consequence of inaction.

## 2. Intervention Quality
**Was what they did an effective and appropriate response?**

Consider ecological appropriateness, execution, difficulty, resourcefulness, efficiency, durability, follow-through and ecological integrity.

## 3. Progress Achieved
**How much did the ecological situation move from its starting position?**

Consider baseline-to-outcome movement, magnitude of improvement, difficulty overcome, additionality, persistence, trajectory and measurable result.

Additionality asks:

> **What changed specifically because this participant acted?**

## 4. System Contribution
**How much does this action strengthen the ecology beyond its immediate footprint?**

Consider ecological connectivity, neighbour benefit, human collaboration, knowledge transfer, leverage, replication, resilience and enabling other ecological activity.

This deliberately rewards helping others. A participant can strengthen their own performance by making another participant, neighbouring property, ecological corridor or community stronger.

# 7. SUPPORTING PERFORMANCE CONCEPTS

Potential sub-criteria/modifiers include:

- **Degree of difficulty:** what was achieved given constraints?
- **Additionality:** would the improvement have occurred without the participant?
- **Durability:** is the improvement likely to last?
- **Leverage:** did a small action enable substantially greater activity?
- **Ecological connectivity:** did it strengthen wider habitat/processes?
- **Human connectivity:** did it connect neighbours, groups, knowledge or resources?
- **Innovation/resourcefulness:** did an effective creative response improve outcomes, replication or leverage?

These need not become equally weighted headline scores.

# 8. EVIDENCE IS NOT PERFORMANCE

Evidence should not become a fifth ecological performance dimension.

Evidence establishes confidence that a performance occurred and can be judged. A participant with expensive cameras, drones or professional communications should not score better merely because their evidence looks better.

The platform should help participants establish sufficient evidence rather than reward production sophistication.

# 9. RECOGNITION AS AN INCENTIVE

Recognition emerged as one of the strongest incentives.

Only a small number can win championships. Thousands can be meaningfully recognised.

Possible recognition includes standout of the week, rookie performance, community achievement, persistence, innovation, collaboration, youth achievement, comeback, neighbour contribution, field diary, moment of the week, wildlife capture, local hero and unsung contributor.

Recognition is not necessarily championship currency. It is a separate source of visibility, pride, storytelling and motivation.

# 10. REGIONAL COMPETITION

Regional identity is a potentially powerful participation and audience mechanism. New Zealanders can be strongly parochial; Ex Games should use that constructively.

Every participant belongs to a place and can contribute to their region.

The regional tally is distinct from individual Competitor performance.

**Do not artificially normalise population away.**

Auckland's population is both an advantage and part of the scale of its ecological challenge. Different regions possess different advantages, constraints and opportunities. Those asymmetries should become part of the competition rather than being removed through opaque coefficients.

If very large numbers of Aucklanders join and care about restoration, that is a success, not a competitive defect.

# 11. CONNECTION AND REGIONAL POINTS

Connecting may generate a **small, one-time regional contribution**.

It is not ecological impact; it is participation/momentum.

> **Joining matters.**

Possible regional contributions include Connection, becoming Active, verified ecological activity, sustained activity, helping another participant, bringing another person into genuine activity, completing a challenge, qualifying as a Competitor, Competitor performance and finals performance.

Exact values are unresolved. Ecological activity and performance should be materially more valuable than registration.

The audience should be able to understand why a regional tally moved.

# 12. ONLINE ENGAGEMENT

Ex Games wants people actively using Ex Games, so online activity needs incentives.

However, social-media skill, likes, posting volume or screen time must not buy ecological championship success.

Online participation should help recognition, connection, mobilisation, storytelling, following, knowledge transfer, recruitment, resource discovery and collaboration.

Digital behaviour is particularly valuable when it causes real-world ecological activity.

# 13. COLLECTIVE GOALS AND CHALLENGES

Competition need not be the only game.

A season may include national/regional collective objectives. Participants can simultaneously compete individually, improve their own baseline, represent their region, help neighbours and cooperate toward national ecological objectives.

Changing focus can provide variety and allow different participants to shine. Possible themes include Water, Predators, Native Plants, Connection, Youth, Waste, Coastal, Wetlands and Wildcard/Your Place.

# 14. UNLOCKS

Unlocks are preferred where they **increase the participant's ability to produce further ecological impact**.

Examples include native plants, traps, field equipment, ecological survey, expert advice, drone mapping, volunteer mobilisation, training and specialist support.

A simple loyalty model — points exchanged for free products someone would otherwise buy — risks sponsor cannibalisation.

A stronger model is an **Impact Unlock Pool** where sponsor resources enable additional work that would otherwise be constrained.

# 15. PARTICIPANT-GENERATED MEDIA

Participant-generated audiovisual material remains a core architectural assumption.

Participants principally film their own activity using phones, inexpensive lavalier microphones, photographs, field cameras, drone footage where available, before/after material and field diaries.

Ex Games obtains appropriate agreed usage rights.

A possible capture grammar:

1. Show the place.
2. Explain what you are trying to change.
3. Show the work.
4. Explain what went wrong or unexpectedly well.
5. Show the result.
6. Explain what happens next.

# 16. WEEKLY MEDIA PRODUCT

The current concept is a weekly approximately **30–40 minute Ex Games programme**.

Possible AI-supported workflow:

**ingest → transcription → indexing → story detection → selects → rough assembly → narration draft → graphics/map context → scoring/standings → human editorial review → final programme**

Human editorial control remains important.

The people doing the work tell their own stories. Ex Games finds, contextualises, verifies, edits, recognises and distributes them.

# 17. REGIONAL EDITORIAL FOCUS

The national competition continues everywhere while the weekly programme can focus on a region or wherever the strongest stories emerge.

A standout need not be selected in advance.

> **Performance produces the stars.**

One week Jo in Tauranga may be the standout; another week Steve in Rotorua. Rookies and seasoned competitors provide recurring sporting narratives.

# 18. DAILY AND WEEKLY MEDIA RHYTHMS

**Daily/continual:** short participant moments, before/after, wildlife, discoveries, failures, humour, milestones and recognition.

**Weekly:** polished 30–40 minute programme creating characters, narrative, rivalry, standings, ecological understanding and fandom.

Working principle:

**Short form acquires attention.  
Weekly programming builds loyalty.  
The Living Map converts attention into connection and participation.**

# 19. EX GAMES AS ITS OWN MEDIA CHANNEL

Conventional broadcasters are not required at launch.

Ex Games can distribute through its own web platform and appropriate external digital/social channels.

The objective is to demonstrate a real audience rather than ask a broadcaster to speculate whether one might exist.

A figure such as 100,000 weekly followers/views was discussed only illustratively, not as a settled target.

More useful evidence includes recurring viewers, episode completion, followers, Connected users, Active participants, submissions, engagement and growth rate.

> **Media attention should be an outcome of proving Ex Games works, not a prerequisite for making Ex Games work.**

If Ex Games builds a large direct audience, Ex Games itself is a media property.

# 20. MEDIA AND PARTICIPATION FLYWHEEL

**Real ecological activity → stories → audience → Ex Games platform → following/connection/participation → more ecological activity → better stories → larger audience.**

Media is not simply monetisation. It can be a primary growth mechanism.

# 21. SCALE AND AUDIENCE ACQUISITION

A few thousand ecological participants alone are unlikely to provide sufficient commercial scale.

Ex Games needs to convert a concentrated actor network into a much larger engaged audience — potentially tens or hundreds of thousands.

Mechanisms include participant communities/social networks, schools, workplaces, regional identity, local media, recognition sharing, short-form media, weekly programming, the Living Map, regional standings and recruitment.

Every ecological actor can become a distribution node into an existing community.

Spectatorship is legitimate participation. A person may follow their region, back local participants, watch, share, Connect, help occasionally and later become Active.

# 22. LOCATED POPULATION AS INFRASTRUCTURE

The nationwide Located population is more than map content.

It demonstrates the latent playing field and supports acquisition, storytelling and commercial credibility.

The Bay of Plenty benchmark currently contains approximately **220 Located ecological actors** following the bounded discovery/reconciliation work completed to date.

A future sponsor proposition becomes materially stronger when Ex Games can truthfully say, for example:

> We have 220 Located operations in your region.  
> 185 have Connected.  
> 143 are Active.  
> They are undertaking identifiable ecological activity across your region.

Exact figures must always be measured and reported truthfully.

# 23. CORE POPULATION METRICS

Keep distinct:

- **Located:** independently identified legitimate work.
- **Connected:** actor has claimed/confirmed and joined.
- **Active:** participating during the relevant period.
- **Qualifying:** seeking to earn Competitor status.
- **Competitor:** qualified for the Games.
- **Following:** audience following people, places, regions and/or Games.

At scale these are not merely analytics; they become part of the commercial proposition.

# 24. PARTICIPANT ACQUISITION — RECOGNITION FIRST

Located actors should not be approached as people who need to be taught to care about ecology. They are already doing the work.

A working line is:

> **We've put your work on the map.**

The wording is not final. The intent is important.

The work is the subject. The participant is the hero. Ex Games is the frame that makes the work visible and connects it to a larger picture.

# 25. NARRATIVE ORDER

Before asking somebody to sign up, Ex Games needs to answer:

1. What is Ex Games?
2. What is it trying to achieve?
3. What's in it for me?
4. What is my place in it?
5. What can I do next?

Only then should requests such as Claim, Connect, Follow, Upload and Compete appear.

Narrative sequence:

**WHAT IT IS → WHY IT EXISTS → WHAT CHANGES → YOUR PLACE IN IT → WHAT YOU GET → WHAT YOU CAN DO → INVITATION**

Public wording remains unresolved.

# 26. VALUE FOR EXISTING ECOLOGICAL ACTORS

Potential value includes making work visible, telling the story, recognition, connecting with nearby work, finding helpers/resources, helping neighbours, sharing knowledge, representing the region, reaching an audience, unlocking additional capacity and optionally qualifying/competing.

The proposition is not “What can you do for Ex Games?”

It is closer to:

> **You are already creating value. Ex Games can help that work travel further.**

# 27. VALUE FOR EVERYONE ELSE

A person need not restore or compete to participate.

They may follow their locality, follow a participant, back their region, watch the programme, share stories, help when convenient, attend an activity, join a challenge, Connect or eventually become Active.

> **You do not have to compete to play a part in Ex Games.**

# 28. SHARING AND ORGANIC DISTRIBUTION

Generic “share Ex Games” requests are weak.

Sharing should celebrate the participant/work.

Possible share objects:

> **OUR WORK IS ON THE MAP**

or:

> **MY WORK IS ON THE MAP**

Recognition therefore doubles as organic distribution infrastructure.

Connected participants may also help answer:

> **Who have we missed?**

# 29. COMMERCIAL MODEL — CORE PRINCIPLE

Ex Games should not be conceived merely as a sponsorship-funded conservation platform.

It has potential to become a multi-sided ecological participation and media economy.

Working commercial proposition:

> **Ex Games creates organised, measurable ecological participation at scale, and organisations pay to activate, support, understand and associate themselves with that activity.**

# 30. EARLY MONETISATION

Likely early sources:

- national/season sponsorship;
- regional sponsorship;
- round/challenge partnerships;
- finals/recognition partnerships;
- sponsored challenges and Impact Unlock Pools;
- grants/philanthropic funding;
- direct digital media value.

Conventional media rights/licensing become more attractive after Ex Games demonstrates a meaningful recurring audience.

# 31. LATER COMMERCIAL OPPORTUNITIES

Potential later streams include corporate participation, council/government programme delivery, supplier marketplace/referrals, supporter membership, events, field/finals experiences, advertising, programme licensing, broadcast/streaming rights, measurement/verification services, aggregated ecological intelligence and international format/IP licensing.

These are opportunities, not commitments.

# 32. CORPORATE PARTICIPATION

Companies may pay for employee ecological challenges, regional participation, internal team competition, volunteering, impact dashboards, matched funding, community participation and credible outcome reporting.

Commercial participation must never purchase championship advantage.

# 33. ECOLOGICAL MARKETPLACE

At scale Ex Games may know what projects require: plants, traps, fencing, equipment, expertise, volunteers, transport, monitoring and mapping.

Suppliers possess those resources.

Ex Games may eventually facilitate matching/transactions and earn referral or marketplace revenue without necessarily carrying inventory.

# 34. ECOLOGICAL INTELLIGENCE

Over time Ex Games could develop an aggregated picture of what ecological activity is happening, where, against which problems, which interventions are attempted, what resources are involved, what outcomes follow and where gaps exist.

This must not become a business based on selling personal participant data.

The opportunity is aggregated ecological intelligence, research and decision support.

# 35. COMMERCIAL AND COMPETITIVE INTEGRITY

Future boundaries include:

- sponsors cannot buy championship results;
- funders cannot buy judging outcomes;
- paying participants cannot obtain competitive advantage;
- slick media cannot substitute for ecological performance;
- editorial independence should be protected;
- participant media rights must be explicit and fair;
- ecological evidence and commercial claims must remain credible.

Ex Games should retain control of its core brand, competition format, participant network, Living Map, data architecture and appropriate media/IP rights.

# 36. MONEY / VALUE FLOW TEST

For every monetisation idea ask:

> **What moves between the parties?**

and:

> **Why would the payer keep paying?**

Actors include Participant, Audience, Ex Games, Sponsor, Supplier, Funder, Corporate, Council/Government, Media Partner and Community/Region.

Revenue ideas without a durable answer to the second question are not core mechanisms.

# 37. MULTILINGUAL ARCHITECTURE

No final language implementation decision was made.

Architectural principle:

> **Ex Games should not be designed as an English-only system.**

Potential future experiences include English, te reo Māori, Mandarin Chinese, Hindi, Gagana Samoa, Lea Faka-Tonga and other languages according to community need.

AI can assist translation, transcription, subtitles and potentially dubbing.

Core te reo Māori terminology and kaupapa should receive appropriate fluent/native-speaker review. Te reo Māori should not simply be treated as another generic translation option in the Aotearoa context.

No multilingual launch scope is agreed. The immediate requirement is to avoid architecture that makes multilingual support unnecessarily difficult later.

# 38. WORKING PRODUCT HIERARCHY

**PURPOSE:** Accelerate ecological restoration and impact, including helping build momentum toward Predator Free 2050 and wider ecological recovery.

**EX GAMES:** Makes ecological restoration and the people doing it visible, connected, engaging and recognised.

**THE GAMES:** Highlight and recognise outstanding restoration through fair competition.

**THE LIVING MAP:** Shows where the work is happening and connects it to place.

**THE NETWORK:** Allows people to Connect, follow, help, participate and represent their region.

**THE MEDIA:** Tells the stories, creates personalities and builds an audience around the work.

**THE INCENTIVES:** Recognition, regional contribution, challenges, qualification, competition and unlocks encourage greater and better activity.

**THE COMMERCIAL MODEL:** Brings audience, sponsorship, funding, resources and eventually economic activity into the ecosystem in ways that can support more ecological impact.

# 39. CURRENT GROWTH FLYWHEEL

**Locate existing ecological actors → recognise their work → explain Ex Games and its purpose → invite them to Connect → participants share with existing communities → communities follow people and places → activity generates short-form stories → standouts feed the weekly programme → programme creates personalities and regional rivalry → regional tallies drive sharing and local interest → audience enters the Living Map → audience discovers nearby activity → some Follow → some Connect → some become Active → some Qualify → some become Competitors → rookies and established competitors create stronger stories → more audience and participation → more ecological impact → faster ecological restoration.**

# 42. PRINCIPLES TO PRESERVE

1. Accelerating ecological restoration and impact is the objective.
2. Predator Free 2050 is an important national challenge Ex Games can help accelerate, but does not define the full ecological scope.
3. Great work already exists; Ex Games begins with recognition rather than admonition.
4. Recognition is a major incentive.
5. Individuals must be capable of outperforming large organisations.
6. Performance is judged in ecological context, not by scale alone.
7. Helping another participant can strengthen your own performance.
8. Evidence establishes confidence; it does not constitute ecological merit.
9. Everyone can participate; competition is voluntary.
10. Competitor status is earned.
11. Regional identity should encourage participation rather than be mathematically equalised away.
12. Connecting can contribute modestly to a region; ecological action contributes much more.
13. Online activity should amplify real-world impact rather than reward screen time.
14. Recognition can be abundant even though championship victory is scarce.
15. Unlocks should preferentially enable additional ecological impact.
16. Participant-generated media is a core production assumption.
17. Ex Games should be capable of becoming its own media property.
18. Broadcaster participation is not a launch dependency.
19. Participants are the heroes; Ex Games provides the stage, connection and recognition.
20. The Located population is ecological infrastructure, acquisition infrastructure and commercial evidence.
21. Measure Located, Connected, Active, Qualifying, Competing and Following distinctly.
22. The Games highlight and recognise outstanding restoration; Ex Games is broader than the Games.
23. Ex Games should not be architected as English-only.
24. Working copy must not accidentally become settled brand language.

# 43. WORKING NORTH STAR

**Find the work already happening.  
Make it visible.  
Recognise it.  
Connect it.  
Tell the stories.  
Give everyone a way to participate.  
Give outstanding performers a Games worth competing in.  
Build an audience around them.  
Use that audience, competition and commercial energy to enable more ecological impact.  
Help the work happen faster.**
